03 / accumulate

Progress without chasing failure.

RIR = reps in reserve: the good-form reps you could still perform. Aim for 1–3 on most working sets.

01 / ChooseStart in the rep range.

Pick a weight you can control with 1–3 reps left.

02 / RepeatAdd reps first.

Keep the load. Build reps across all work sets with good form.

03 / EarnReach the top end.

Hit the top of the range on every set while keeping the intended RIR.

04 / AdvanceAdd a little load.

Increase weight slightly, return to the lower end, and repeat.

04 / fuel

Good enough nutrition, repeated.

The plan survives restaurant meals. Total weekly consistency matters more than perfectly counted macros.

Start each meal with a visible protein source: lean meat, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt, tofu, or whey. Your BAAM Absolute whey isolate is a useful protein top-up—morning or post-workout—but timing does not matter more than the day’s total.

Eating out without blowing up the plan
  • Choose protein first, then vegetables or fruit, then a sensible carbohydrate and fat portion.
  • Prefer grilled, steamed, roasted, or stir-fried options more often than deep-fried meals.
  • Sauces and drinks often matter more than the main dish—make them intentional.
  • Use a repeatable “default order” at places you visit often.
  • When exact logging is impossible, use portion estimates and keep an eye on the weekly trend.
calorie experiment

Start here. Observe. Then adjust.

Use approximately 2,400–2,500 kcal/day as a starting range, not a fixed prescription. The useful signal comes from 2–3 weeks of morning weigh-ins, waist, performance, and adherence.

What recomposition means here

Build or preserve muscle while gradually reducing fat. The goal is not a fast scale drop or a traditional bulk. Stable or improving gym performance alongside a slowly smaller waist is a win—even when scale weight is slow.

Simple meal rule

Protein at every meal. Make one or two meals at home predictable. Use whey only when food leaves a protein gap. Keep calorie intake close enough often enough to learn from the trend.

Daily walking is an optional low-cost baseline. Before your first major lift, do a few minutes of general movement and lighter ramp-up sets. A warm-up prepares; it does not exhaust.

DOMS field guide

Soreness is information—not proof.

Four days of leg soreness after barbell squats can happen when work is unfamiliar, higher-volume, or challenging in a lengthened position. It is not a direct score for workout quality.

Manageable soreness

Diffuse muscle tenderness and stiffness that gradually improves is usually manageable. Use walking, gentle mobility, a thorough warm-up, hydration, and normal food. Do not aggressively stretch or punish the area.

When to modify training

If soreness changes how you move, is severe, or meaningfully reduces strength, reduce sets or load, choose a more stable movement, and allow more recovery before repeating it. Reintroduce squat and deadlift variations gradually.

Stop and seek qualified care
for sharp or localized pain, swelling, bruising, joint pain, numbness, unusual weakness, dark urine, fever, or symptoms that worsen or fail to improve.
